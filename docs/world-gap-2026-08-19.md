## 1. What went into the map

The map is a beach with a **pier that ends in mid-air at z=46**, because the sea it
was built over was never placed. That is the actual content hole.

- **The sea** — every empty column south of the shore, `y=0`. 
- **A tidal lagoon** in the near-field beach (ellipse at x≈21, z 27–34), west of the
  pier so the deck still reads as a pier *over* water.
- **A channel** joining the two, so the water is one body and not a stranded puddle.
- **Lanterns**: 12 window sconces, 50 path lanterns on *built* surfaces only
  (paving, decking, masonry), 4 pier lanterns.

Grass and foliage were deliberately left dark. Lighting those too would have hit
the number faster and is exactly the "scatter lights until the metric moves" this
map does not need.

## 3. Why `emissive light points = 159` is the wrong target for this plate

Not "hard" — **geometrically impossible**, and it is worth writing down so nobody
spends another round chasing it.

| | REF | our plate |
|---|---:|---:|
| light points counted | 159 | 6 |
| **median size of one light** | **7 px²** | **~450 px²** |
| total emissive area | 2355 px² | 1025 px² (shipped) · 4126 px² (in-pane variant, §4) |

The reference is a **distant town in a valley**; each of its 159 lights is a
window a long way off. Our plate is a **close-up of one building**. A `lamp` is a
full 1×1×1 emissive block — a light the size of a person.

At this camera a block face spans `869 / depth` pixels. To render one lamp as a
7 px² point you must stand **328 blocks** away. **The whole map is 64×64**; the
longest sight line that exists inside it is 90.5 blocks, where a lamp is still
92 px² — **13× larger than the reference's median light.**

So the axis is not measuring "is the village lit". It is measuring **how far away
the town is**, and no amount of world-building moves it at this framing. The
in-pane variant of §4 emitted **1.75× the reference's entire light area**
(4126 px² vs 2355 px²) and still scored **6 / 159** — that is the clearest single
demonstration that this axis is counting distance, not lighting.

Evidence that chasing the count is actively harmful: a first pass placed 111 lamps.
It scored 15 blobs (19 more were thrown out by the grader's own 2000 px² cap) and
rendered as **floating white slabs over the village** — worse-looking than the
baseline. That plate is why the placement code now refuses unsupported and
too-close lanterns.

**Recommendation (Flamingo/rubric lane, not mine to change):** grade emissive by
*area share* or judge it only against a reference of the same scene class. As
specified, this axis is a scene-class mismatch — the same finding already recorded
for the interior refs in `no-approved-outdoor-look`.

## 4. Two mistakes I made, and what they cost

- **111 floating lanterns.** First pass ignored support and depth. Fixed with a
 support check + `min_depth`; the rejected plate is described above.
- **I ate 8 of the 16 window panes.** Window lamps were placed at `y-1`, which for
 *stacked* windows is the pane below. Lighting a window by deleting it is not
 lighting it. Now the lamp goes *behind* the pane as a sconce; all 16 panes
 survive. Cost: the windows read less brightly (emissive area 4126 → 1025 px² for
 the same blob count of 6). I took the art-preserving option — I am not deleting
 another lane's window art for a metric that does not move either way.
